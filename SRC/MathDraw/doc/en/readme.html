Math Draw

Math Draw turns a typed description of a shape into an actual drawing, and then describes that drawing back to you. Everything happens on your own machine - there is no internet connection involved and nothing is sent anywhere.

Press NVDA+Alt+D to open it. You can change that from NVDA's Input Gestures dialog, under the Math Draw category.

Drawing something

Type what you want in the description box and press the Draw button. Plain English works best - "a circle of radius 5" rather than anything formal. When the drawing is ready you'll hear a short high beep and a summary of what was made.

Shift+Up and Shift+Down move back and forth through descriptions you have used before, so you don't have to retype a long one.

What you can ask for

Shapes

Circle, square, rectangle, triangle, parallelogram, rhombus, pentagon, hexagon and octagon. Numbers in the description are used as measurements:

  • a circle of radius 5
  • a square of side 10
  • a rectangle 8 by 4
  • a triangle 10 6
  • a right triangle 6 8
  • a parallelogram 10 6
  • a rhombus 10 6
  • a regular hexagon 5

For triangles and parallelograms the two numbers are the base and the height, in that order. Say "right triangle" if you want the right angle drawn in the corner; otherwise you get an isosceles one.

"Box" is understood as a rectangle and "round" as a circle. Common misspellings of triangle are picked up too. If you leave the numbers out, a sensible default size is used.

One shape inside another

  • a circle inside a square
  • a square inscribed in a circle

Saying "inscribed" or "touching" makes the inner shape as large as it can be while still fitting. Otherwise it is drawn at half size.

Coordinates

Write coordinate pairs as numbers separated by a comma and they will be plotted on a pair of axes, labelled A, B, C and so on:

  • plot the points 3,4 and -2,5
  • draw a coordinate plane

Angles and graphs

  • an angle of 60 degrees
  • a parabola
  • a sine wave
  • a cosine curve

Number lines

These need the words "number line" in the description:

  • represent square root of 5 on number line - draws the geometric construction, not just a dot
  • represent 3/4 on number line - marks the fraction with the line divided into quarters
  • represent 2.35 on number line - shows a second, zoomed in line underneath so you can see where the decimal falls

Exploring a drawing

A summary is spoken as soon as a drawing is finished, but the Explore list is where the detail is. Tab to it and use the arrow keys to move through the figure one element at a time. Each element is described - "circle, radius 190 pixels, centred in the centre", "line from the top left to the bottom right" - and given a short beep at the same time.

The beep tells you where the element sits. A high note means near the top of the drawing and a low note means near the bottom. The sound moves to your left or right ear depending on which side of the drawing the element is on. With headphones on you can get a feel for the layout fairly quickly.

Saving and copying

Save image writes the drawing to a file. PNG and JPEG are ordinary pictures, saved at four times the size shown on screen so they stay sharp when enlarged or printed. SVG saves it as a vector, which can be scaled to any size at all and reopened in a drawing program later.

Copy image puts the drawing on the clipboard so you can paste it straight into a document or an email. The description is attached as alternative text, so anyone reading it with a screen reader gets the description too.

Drawing from the camera

Draw from Camera points a webcam at a shape on paper or a board, works out what it is, and offers to redraw it. The preview tells you how clear the picture is, so you can steady the camera before taking it.

This part needs OpenCV, which is too large to ship inside the add-on. The first time you press Draw from Camera you will be asked whether you want to download it - it is around 55 MB. Nothing is downloaded unless you say yes.

The download runs in the background, so you can carry on describing and drawing shapes while it works. A progress bar shows how far along it is and the percentage is spoken every ten percent. You can cancel at any point; nothing is installed unless the whole download finishes and passes its checksum.

This only happens once. After that the files are kept in your NVDA configuration folder and Math Draw is fully offline again, camera included.

If a description isn't understood

You'll hear a low beep and a message saying so. Nothing is saved when that happens, so you can just reword it and try again. Naming the shape directly is usually enough to get it working - "a triangle" will always draw something, where "the thing from question 4" won't.
